Get help

You do not have to deal with everything alone.

Beyond the Mud is an educational project and cannot replace a psychologist, therapist, doctor, social worker, or emergency service. If you need professional help, these resources can help you find somewhere to start.

If you are in immediate danger

112

European emergency number. If you are in immediate danger and need urgent assistance, call 112 where available.

For Ukraine, emergency services also include:

  • 112: unified emergency number
  • 102: police
  • 103: emergency medical assistance
  • 101: rescue service

If you are a child or teenager and need someone to talk to

National Child and Youth Hotline, Ukraine

116 111 from mobile phones

0 800 500 225 from mobile or landline

The service provides anonymous support and advice concerning relationships, bullying, difficulties with parents, violence and other difficult situations.

If you are experiencing violence or feel unsafe at home

Government Hotline, Ukraine

1547

A national hotline dealing with:

  • domestic violence
  • violence against children
  • gender-based violence
  • human trafficking
  • threats of violence

The service can provide information and psychological support and can register reports concerning violence.

If you are struggling emotionally

Mental-health and psychosocial support, Ukraine

0 800 500 335

116 123

UNICEF's Ukraine resource information lists these contacts for mental-health and psychosocial support.

Crisis and suicide-prevention lines by country

If you are thinking about suicide, or you feel you cannot cope, you can reach out right now. Check the details for your country below.

United States

988

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Call or text 988.

Online chat

Ukraine

7333

Lifeline Ukraine. National suicide prevention and mental health support line. Listed as a 24/7 service in Ukrainian government resources.

England

111

NHS urgent mental health support. Call 111 and select the mental health option. Available 24/7 for urgent mental health support, including for children and teenagers.

Canada

9-8-8

Call or text 9-8-8 for suicide crisis support, 24/7.

For young people aged 5 to 29, Kids Help Phone is also available at 1-800-668-6868 or by texting CONNECT to 686868.

Australia

13 11 14

Lifeline Australia offers crisis support 24/7.

Young people aged 5 to 25 can contact Kids Helpline at 1800 55 1800.

For immediate emergencies, call 000.

New Zealand

1737

Call or text 1737 for free support from a trained counsellor, 24/7.

For immediate emergencies, call 111.

Ireland

116 123

Samaritans provides free emotional support, 24/7.

For emergencies, call 112 or 999.

India

14416

Tele-MANAS provides national tele-mental-health support. Another listed contact is 1-800-891-4416.

For immediate emergencies, call 112.

112 and 911 are general emergency numbers in many countries, not necessarily dedicated mental health services.

If you are outside Ukraine

Emergency and child-support services differ between countries. UNICEF maintains country-specific information for children and families, including emergency numbers, child helplines, anti-violence services and mental-health support.

Examples

  • Poland: emergency 112 · child helpline 116 111 · anti-violence helpline 116 123
  • Czech Republic: emergency 112 · child helpline 116 111 · anti-violence helpline 116 006
  • Romania: emergency 112 · child helpline 116 111 · anti-violence helpline 0800 500 333
  • Moldova: emergency 112 · child helpline 116 111 · anti-violence helpline 0800 88 008
  • Slovakia: emergency 112 · child helpline 116 111

For the most current numbers, use UNICEF's country-by-country resource rather than relying on this list alone.

You can also talk to someone you trust

Professional help is not the only possible source of support. If you are not in immediate danger, consider reaching out to:

  • a school counselor
  • a teacher
  • a relative
  • a trusted family friend
  • a psychologist or therapist
  • a doctor
  • a youth-support organization

You don't need to wait until a situation becomes unbearable before asking someone for help.

One more thing

You don't have to know exactly what is wrong before asking for help. You can simply say:

"Things at home have been difficult, and I don't know what to do."

That is enough to start a conversation.

If you are in immediate danger, use an emergency service rather than waiting for an online resource to respond.
